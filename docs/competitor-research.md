## Synthesis

Expected on nearly every site: full-bleed photo or video hero with one "Book Now"; How It Works in 3 or 4 steps; fleet grid; What's Included; FAQ; testimonials; phone in header or footer; age and licence line; passenger note; gift-voucher path.

Five most common mistakes:
1. Hiding prices or showing only "Starting at".
2. Booking that ends in "email or call us".
3. Fleet shown as brand names, not specific cars with year and spec.
4. Reviews block with no number.
5. Missing cancellation, weather, and damage-liability policy.

To beat Sunset: three prices in one row on the landing page; real availability instead of PayPal-then-phone; named cars with specs and Apex's own photography; one hero line and one CTA; a named route with a map (only Gotham does this; Apex's route-line motion is unique among all seven); a ratings strip with platform and count; a Know Before You Book block; an audience wider than "for him".

Pricing: the ladder is almost linear ($16.50/min at 30, $16.58/min at 60), so the lever is a "Most Popular" badge on the 45-minute tier and listing what changes between tiers (miles, route sections, photos or video) rather than only minutes. State passenger-free and insurance-included on the card. Show custom as "From $" with an enquiry CTA. No strikethrough discounts.

Trust signals for a public-road product, in order: insurance included and the exact deductible or liability cap; age and licence up front; instructor in the car and what they do; cancellation and weather policy; waiver named and linked; passenger policy; real reviews with platform and count; photos of real customers in the real cars on the real road; a physical meeting address and phone.
